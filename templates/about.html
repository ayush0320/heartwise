About {{ cfg.PROJECT_TITLE }}

An explainable heart-disease screening app, built as a B.Tech project to go beyond "a model that outputs a number".

The problem

Heart disease is a leading cause of death worldwide and is often found late, because the tests that confirm it are expensive and need a specialist. The measurements used here (blood pressure, cholesterol, ECG, exercise response) are already collected at a normal check-up, so a low-cost screening aid is realistic.

Most student projects stop at a notebook with an accuracy figure. This one adds explanations, a test on other hospitals, and a full application around the model.

What is new compared with a basic classifier

  • SHAP explanations for every prediction, in percentage points
  • Cross-hospital validation: trained on Cleveland, tested on three other hospitals
  • Screening mode, a lower threshold chosen to fix the low recall that validation exposed
  • A product around it: patients, health log, reports, API, batch scoring, drift monitoring, model versions

Limitations, stated plainly

  • 303 training patients from one hospital in the 1980s, with few women. The test set is only 61 patients, so scores can move several points with a different split.
  • The model ranks patients reasonably on other hospitals but its 50% cut-off does not transfer. Screening mode helps, at the cost of more false alarms.
  • Other hospitals' data have 20-30% missing values, filled with Cleveland medians. Some of the gap may come from the data and not the model.
  • SHAP explains the model, not the disease. Nothing here has been clinically validated.
  • This version has no user login. Do not enter real personal data on a public deployment.
